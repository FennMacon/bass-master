Bass basics

Pitch, rhythm, and the fretboard

Start by connecting what you hear to what you play. Pitch tells you how high or low a note sounds, and rhythm tells you when it starts and how long it lasts. As the bass player, you help the band feel both.

  • Read the bass staff
  • Count note lengths
  • Feel the meter
  • Leave space with rests
  • Hold notes with dots and ties
  • Move by steps
  • Practice
  • Reference diagrams

Read the bass staff

A staff has five lines and four spaces. The clef gives those positions their note names. The two dots of the bass clef sit around the F line, fourth from the bottom.

  • Lines, bottom to top: G, B, D, F, A.
  • Spaces, bottom to top: A, C, E, G.

Ledger lines extend the staff for notes above or below it. You’ll see them frequently in bass music because the lowest strings sit below the staff.

Count note lengths

A note’s shape marks its duration. In 4/4, a quarter note lasts one beat, so the other durations fit around it like this:

  • Whole: 4 beats. Half: 2 beats.
  • Quarter: 1 beat. Eighth: ½ beat. Sixteenth: ¼ beat.

Tap four steady beats and say “1 & 2 & 3 & 4 &.” Each number and each “&” marks an eighth note.

Feel the meter

Barlines divide music into measures, also called bars. A time signature tells you how the beats in those measures are grouped.

  • 4/4: Four quarter-note beats. Count “1, 2, 3, 4.”
  • 3/4: Three quarter-note beats. Count “1, 2, 3.”
  • 6/8: Six eighth notes grouped into two dotted-quarter beats. Count “ONE-and-a TWO-and-a.”

Leave space with rests

A rest tells you how long to stay silent. Keep counting through it and mute the string so the previous note stops ringing.

A whole rest hangs from a staff line; a half rest sits on top of one. A quarter rest looks like a squiggle, and eighth and sixteenth rests have one and two flags respectively.

Hold notes with dots and ties

A dot adds half the original duration. In 4/4, a dotted half note lasts three beats: two for the half note and one for the dot.

A tie connects notes of the same pitch into one sound. In 4/4, two tied quarter notes last two beats, even across a barline. Pluck only the first note.

Move by steps

On a single string, a half step is one fret and a whole step is two. Go up the fret numbers to raise the pitch.

A sharp (♯) raises a natural note by a half step, a flat (♭) lowers it by a half step, and a natural (♮) restores its unaltered pitch. In a key signature, the sharps or flats are the default for that letter name.

Keep time on one string

  1. Tap or set a metronome to a comfortable pace, about 60 beats per minute.
  2. On the open E string, play four quarter notes while counting “1, 2, 3, 4.”
  3. Play on beats 1 and 3 and mute for beats 2 and 4. Count aloud through the rests.

Listen for: An even pulse and clean silence on beats 2 and 4.
