Scales and key signatures

Find the notes in a key

A scale is a set of notes arranged by pitch. Learn the pattern of steps between them, and you can build the same kind of scale from any starting note.

  • Build a major scale
  • Compare three minor scales
  • Number the scale degrees
  • Read a key signature
  • Find the major key
  • Practice

Build a major scale

Pick a starting note, then follow whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half. A whole step is two frets on one string and a half step is one.

C major: C, D, E, F, G, A, B, C. The half steps are E to F and B to C.

A fretted scale shape can move along the neck. If your shape uses an open string, you’ll need to adjust the fingering to move it.

Compare three minor scales

  • Natural minor: Whole, half, whole, whole, half, whole, whole. A natural minor is A, B, C, D, E, F, G, A.
  • Harmonic minor: Raise the seventh note of natural minor. In A, change G to G♯.
  • Melodic minor: In the classical form, raise the sixth and seventh on the way up, then use natural minor on the way down. Jazz commonly uses the raised form in both directions.

Number the scale degrees

Number the notes 1 through 7 from the starting note. Degree 1 is the tonic, your home note. In C major, degree 5 is G, the dominant.

The other names are supertonic (2), mediant (3), subdominant (4), and submediant (6). Degree 7 is a leading tone when it sits a half step below the tonic, or a subtonic when it sits a whole step below, as in natural minor.

Read a key signature

The sharps or flats after the clef set which letter names are sharp or flat by default. An accidental in the music can change a particular note.

A major key and its relative minor share a key signature. C major and A minor have no sharps or flats. Listen for the note or chord that feels like home to tell the keys apart.

On the circle of fifths, start at C major. Moving toward G, D, and A adds sharps. Moving toward F, B♭, and E♭ adds flats.

Find the major key

  • Order of sharps: F♯, C♯, G♯, D♯, A♯, E♯, B♯. The major tonic is a half step up from the last sharp. If you see only F♯, the major key is G.
  • Order of flats: B♭, E♭, A♭, D♭, G♭, C♭, F♭. If there are at least two flats, the second-to-last is the major tonic. One flat is F major.

To find the relative minor, start on degree 6 of the major scale. For example, E minor shares G major’s F♯.

Play two scales with the same notes

On the A string, start C major at fret 3 and A natural minor at the open string. Move up one fret for each half step and two for each whole step. Play back down and listen for which note feels settled in each scale.

Check your frets

C major: 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 12, 14, 15.

A natural minor: 0, 2, 3, 5, 7, 8, 10, 12.
