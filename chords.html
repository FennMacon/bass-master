Chords

Find the notes that outline a chord

You can spell out a chord’s sound on bass by playing its notes one at a time. Start with the root, third, and fifth, then listen to what changes when you add the seventh.

  • Build a triad
  • Choose the lowest note
  • Add a seventh
  • Recognize other seventh chords
  • Invert a seventh chord
  • Practice
  • Reference diagrams

Build a triad

A triad has three distinct notes: a root, a third, and a fifth. Each of these examples starts on C.

  • Major: C–E–G. Minor: C–E♭–G.
  • Diminished: C–E♭–G♭. Augmented: C–E–G♯.

The third is a useful target for a bass line because it distinguishes major from minor while the root and fifth stay the same.

Choose the lowest note

The lowest sounding note determines the chord’s position. C–E–G can be voiced three ways with different bass notes.

  • Root position: C in the bass, written C.
  • First inversion: E in the bass, written C/E.
  • Second inversion: G in the bass, written C/G.

When a chart uses a slash, read the note after it as the bass note. The chord root stays C in all three examples.

Add a seventh

A seventh chord adds a fourth note, a seventh above the root. Listen to how the seventh changes the sound of a major or minor triad.

  • Cmaj7: C–E–G–B. Major triad and major seventh.
  • C7: C–E–G–B♭. Major triad and minor seventh, called a dominant seventh.
  • Cm7: C–E♭–G–B♭. Minor triad and minor seventh.
  • Cm7♭5: C–E♭–G♭–B♭. Diminished triad and minor seventh, called half-diminished.

Recognize other seventh chords

  • Cdim7: C–E♭–G♭–B𝄫. Diminished triad and diminished seventh. B double-flat sounds like A on a fretted bass.
  • Cmaj7♯5: C–E–G♯–B. Augmented triad and major seventh.
  • C7♯5: C–E–G♯–B♭. Augmented triad and minor seventh.

Invert a seventh chord

Seventh chords have one more inversion than triads. Put the third in the bass for first inversion, the fifth for second inversion, or the seventh for third inversion.

Example: C7/B♭ is in third inversion because B♭ is the seventh of C7. That bass note can help lead smoothly down a step to A in the next chord.

Play the chord one note at a time

  1. Play C on the third fret of the A string, E on the second fret of the D string, then G on the open G string.
  2. Play the same pattern with E♭ on the first fret of the D string and hear how it changes.
  3. Return to C–E–G, then add B♭ on the third fret of the G string.
Check the chord names

You played C major, C minor, then C7.
